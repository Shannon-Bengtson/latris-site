Scientific basis

Reference

Methods and foundations reference.

The compact reference, mirrored from the open-source repository: for each method, the literature it stands on, what Latris implements and its limits. Every Latris report's methods section is generated from the same registry. For the fuller account of each model type, start at the scientific basis.

Latris implements established methods and cites them. It does not invent a new causal calculus, a new simulation algorithm, or a new theory of evidence; it implements well-known ones, records which were used in each project, and states their limits next to their results. This page is the map: for each formalism, the literature it stands on, what Latris actually implements (with the command or module that implements it), and what it deliberately does not do.

The citation rule. A work is cited only where Latris implements the method it describes, not the whole book, and never a method that is only planned. Where Latris borrows an idea as inspiration rather than implementation, the text says so. If you find a citation here that overclaims, that is a bug: please report it.

One substrate, several formalisms. Every model type in Latris shares the same evidentiary substrate: links are claims, claims carry sources, sources are checked against what they are claimed to support, and disagreement is recorded rather than resolved silently. The formalisms below differ in what kind of reasoning they support; the evidence discipline does not.

Choosing between them. The guide by question, "how does this work?" (CLD), "can it be estimated?" (DAG), "how likely?" (BN), "which forces dominate?" (FCM), "how does it behave over time?" (SD), lives at https://latris.app/formalisms/, mirrored for agents in the repo's llms.txt and the server help topic model-types-overview.

Causal loop diagrams (qualitative)

  • Lineage. Sterman (2000), Business Dynamics, ch. 4, the canonical treatment of causal loop diagrams and loop polarity. Meadows (2008), Thinking in Systems, the accessible statement of the same tradition.
  • Implemented. Loop detection by Johnson's (1975) elementary-circuits algorithm (latris analyse loops); polarity classification (an odd count of − edges makes a balancing loop); qualitative effect propagation along paths (operations/graph_analysis.py).
  • Limits. A polarity of ~ (ambiguous) is reported as "unknown", not guessed. Loop enumeration is capped; beyond the cap Latris raises TooManyLoops rather than silently sampling. Decision-rule, governance and definitional edges are excluded from loop and centrality analysis; they are policy or identity links, not feedback mechanisms.

Causal DAGs: identification, then estimation under stated assumptions

  • Lineage. Pearl (2009), Causality; Pearl & Mackenzie (2018), The Book of Why; Hernán & Robins (2020), Causal Inference: What If. The d-separation test follows Shachter (1998), "Bayes-Ball".
  • Implemented. d-separation queries, Markov blankets, path enumeration (with chain/fork/collider classification), and backdoor adjustment sets by graph surgery: the exposure's outgoing edges removed, then testing which sets d-separate exposure from outcome (latris analyse, DAG-scoped).
  • Effect estimation (latris analyse estimate). With a dataset, the average effect of one factor on another by back-door adjustment (Pearl 2009): the smallest valid back-door set made only of measured factors (or a set you give, checked against the DAG), then outcome regression (Hernán & Robins 2020, ch. 15), ordinary least squares with HC3 robust standard errors (MacKinnon & White 1985) for a numeric outcome, logistic regression (odds ratio and average marginal effect) for a two-valued one, via statsmodels (Seabold & Perktold 2010). The unadjusted association is reported beside it. If every valid set needs a factor the dataset doesn't measure, Latris names what to measure and estimates nothing.
  • Limits: read this before citing Pearl. An estimate is causal only if the DAG leaves out no common cause of treatment and outcome, the model's form is right (linear or logistic, additive in the adjustment variables, no effect modification), and positivity holds; the report states these every time. Complete cases only. One numeric or two-valued treatment at a time. No g-computation, inverse-probability weighting, instrumental variables or front-door estimation, and no counterfactual computation anywhere in the package. Minimal adjustment sets are enumerated up to size 3; the search for a measured set goes to size 6. Analyses run on the causal-edge subgraph only.

Bayesian networks

  • Lineage. Pearl (1988), Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems, the founding text. Fenton & Neil (2018), Risk Assessment and Decision Analysis with Bayesian Networks (2nd ed.), the closest in spirit to Latris: expert-elicited structure, defensible numbers, argued cases. Soft (likelihood) evidence follows Jeffrey (1965), The Logic of Decision.
  • Implemented. Exact inference by variable elimination (pgmpy), per-node posterior marginals; soft (likelihood) evidence per node; weights need not sum to one (--likelihood); bn explain for the most probable joint states (MAP, or MPE when all variables are queried) with margins; Noisy-OR canonical models (binary nodes); CPT parameter learning from data (MLE / Bayesian-Dirichlet estimators) with human-elicited rows protected from overwrite and learned rows marked data-derived; one-way (tornado) sensitivity of a target node over CPT cells, with a provenance overlay; probabilistic sensitivity analysis by Monte Carlo over uncertain CPT rows (bn set-uncertainty --n with bn monte-carlo) (latris bn …).
  • Limits. Variable elimination only; no junction tree or belief propagation. Marginals, not joints. Noisy-OR requires binary nodes and parents. Tornado sensitivity is one-way; probabilistic sensitivity analysis ships separately by Monte Carlo, as above; EVPI is deferred. Structure learning is excluded by design; see latris capabilities, excluded_by_design.

Fuzzy cognitive maps

  • Lineage. Kosko (1986), "Fuzzy Cognitive Maps", International Journal of Man-Machine Studies 24(1), weighted matrix iteration to a steady state.
  • Implemented. A ← f(A·W) iterated to convergence with sigmoid (default), tanh-rescaled, or step transfer functions; clamped scenario nodes; weights validated to [−1, +1] (latris fcm …).
  • Limits. Fixed-point analysis only. A run that does not converge is flagged converged=False; limit cycles are not detected or classified.

System dynamics

  • Lineage. Forrester (1961), Industrial Dynamics; Sterman (2000), Business Dynamics; Meadows (2008). Loop dominance follows the Loops That Matter method (Schoenberg, Davidsen & Eberlein (2020), System Dynamics Review), with the revised flow-to-stock link score of Schoenberg, Hayward & Eberlein (2023), the approach Stella and pysimlin ship.
  • Implemented. Stock-and-flow simulation with dimensional analysis (pint; custom base dimensions for person, dollar, item): fixed-step forward Euler, or adaptive RK23/RK45; STEP, RAMP, PULSE, TABLE and arithmetic builtins; SMOOTH/SMOOTH3 information smoothing and DELAY1/DELAY3 material delays, expanded into stock-and-flow structure (Sterman 2000, ch. 11); unit-checked scenario overrides with provenance (latris sd …); uncertainty by seeded Latin-hypercube sampling (McKay, Beckman & Conover 1979) with percentile bands and Spearman rank correlations; one-at-a-time sensitivity; per-step loop dominance scores with dominant loops flagged (latris sd loops).
  • Limits. The adaptive RK option is solve_ivp RK45 with a step cap, not the classical fixed-step RK4 of Vensim/Stella. Loop dominance enumerates loops exhaustively (Johnson's algorithm) up to a cap of 500; larger models would need the strongest-path search, which is not implemented. Scores are zero whenever stocks stop changing; the method explains change, not equilibrium. Monte Carlo is capped at 1000 runs and reports rank correlations only.

Fault trees

  • Lineage. Vesely, Goldberg, Roberts & Haasl (1981), Fault Tree Handbook (NUREG-0492, US Nuclear Regulatory Commission), and IEC 61025:2006. Minimal cut sets by MOCUS-style expansion (Fussell & Vesely 1972); the exact top-event probability from a binary decision diagram (Rauzy 1993); importance after Birnbaum (1969) and Fussell (1975).
  • Implemented. Event types as view roles (top, intermediate, basic, undeveloped); AND, OR and k-out-of-n (vote) gates stored on their events. Minimal cut sets with superset elimination at every gate, so an event that feeds several gates is handled correctly; single points of failure (cut sets of one). When every leaf has a probability: the exact top-event probability from a reduced ordered BDD, with the rare-event approximation and the min-cut upper bound beside it; Birnbaum importance (P(top | e) − P(top | not e)), Fussell–Vesely importance ((P(top) − P(top | not e)) / P(top)) and risk achievement worth (P(top | e) / P(top)), each computed exactly on the diagram. Every probability keeps its origin, source and verification, and important values nobody has verified are flagged (latris ft …).
  • Limits. Static, coherent trees only: no NOT, priority-AND, sequence or other dynamic gates. Basic events are assumed independent, there is no common-cause failure model (beta factor, MGL), so redundancy can look stronger than it is. Uncertainty is propagated by Monte Carlo (below), but there is no conversion from failure rates and exposure times (enter the probability for the period the tree is about). Cut sets are listed up to 20,000; past that, limit the order.

Bow-ties

  • Lineage. CCPS & Energy Institute (2018), Bow Ties in Risk Management: A Concept Book for Process Safety, the elements, the barrier types and the rules for drawing lines; the quantification is layer of protection analysis, CCPS (2001).
  • Implemented. Hazard, top event, threats, consequences, barriers and escalation factors as view roles; lines as ordinary links, checked against the bow-tie pattern. Per barrier: CCPS type, status (in place, degraded, not in place), owner and probability of failure on demand, with provenance. The analysis lists every line with its effective barriers (degraded or missing ones don't count) and flags lines with none or one, barriers on more than one line, escalation factors with no control and barriers with no owner. With every threat's frequency and every barrier's PFD: the top-event frequency (Σ over threats of frequency × probability that some route gets through), each consequence's frequency, and risk achievement worth per barrier. A threat's routes are combined exactly with the fault-tree decision diagram, so a barrier on two routes counts once.
  • Limits. Barriers are assumed to fail independently, shared barriers are flagged, but common-cause failure isn't modelled; LOPA is an order-of-magnitude method; its values are points unless given ranges for Monte Carlo (below). Escalation factors are drawn and checked but not quantified, and there is no consequence severity or risk matrix.

Event trees

  • Lineage. IEC 62502:2010, Event tree analysis; the US NRC PRA Procedures Guide (NUREG/CR-2300, 1983), descending from the Reactor Safety Study (WASH-1400, 1975).
  • Implemented. Initiating event, headings (functional events) and end states as view roles; the heading order and the sequences stored as data. A check that the sequences form a complete tree: at every branch point all sequences ask the same next heading, both answers are covered, and none stops while a sibling continues. P(yes) per heading, in general or given earlier answers (the most specific match applies; two equally specific values that disagree are reported, not guessed). Each sequence's probability is the product of its branch probabilities; with the initiating frequency, its frequency per year; totals per end state with the dominant sequence behind each. Every value keeps its provenance.
  • Limits. Binary branches only; dependence between headings exists only as the conditional probabilities you state; uncertainty only by Monte Carlo over stated ranges (below); heading probabilities are entered (they can cite a fault-tree run) rather than computed from linked fault trees.

Uncertainty in the risk models (Monte Carlo)

  • Lineage. Probabilistic risk assessment propagates parameter uncertainty by sampling (NUREG/CR-2300, ch. 12), with each input's state-of-knowledge distribution usually a lognormal stated by its median and error factor (95th percentile over the median); the epistemic reading of those distributions is Apostolakis (1990). Latin hypercube sampling follows McKay, Beckman & Conover (1979) and Helton & Davis (2003).
  • Implemented. latris ft|bowtie|et uncertainty gives a basic event's probability, a barrier's PFD, a threat's or initiator's frequency, or a heading's (conditional) P(yes) a distribution, lognormal by median and error factor, or uniform, triangular, normal, beta, gamma, kept within [0, 1] for probabilities, with provenance and a reason; the range must include the current value. monte-carlo samples them (seeded Latin hypercube by default) through the type's own calculation, the fault tree's exact decision diagram, the bow-tie's line combination, the event tree's sequence products, and reports each result's 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles and mean beside the point estimate, and Spearman rank correlations for what drives the spread. Each input takes one value per run wherever it appears, so a barrier on two lines or an event under two gates is not treated as two independent copies.
  • Limits. Different inputs are sampled independently: no correlation between, say, two pumps' failure probabilities beyond what a shared event expresses. Inputs without a range are held exact, so the spread is a lower bound. Rank correlation captures monotone influence only; global sensitivity indices are not computed.

Multi-criteria decision analysis

  • Lineage. The additive value model of multi-attribute value theory (Keeney & Raiffa 1976), as set out for practice by Belton & Stewart (2002) and for public-sector appraisal in the UK government's Multi-criteria analysis: a manual (DCLG 2009). Weights are swing weights: how much moving from the worst to the best of each criterion's scale matters, relative to the other criteria's swings.
  • Implemented. Options and criteria as view roles. Each score becomes a value from 0 to 1 on a linear scale between the criterion's worst and best (stated, or the options' range, flagged); values are combined by normalised weights into a total out of 100. The analysis gives the ranking with each criterion's contribution, dominated options, and for each weight the nearest values, the others keeping their proportions, at which the top choice changes (exact, since totals are linear in one weight). It names the scores the winning margin rests on that nobody has verified. Every weight and score keeps its provenance.
  • Limits. Assumes preferential independence and linear value between worst and best; scores outside a stated scale are clipped (and flagged). Weights mean nothing apart from their scales, and scales taken from the options' range shift when an option is added. One-way weight sensitivity only; no probabilistic weights or scores; no AHP, outranking (ELECTRE, PROMETHEE) or TOPSIS.

Analysis of competing hypotheses

  • Lineage. Heuer (1999), Psychology of Intelligence Analysis, ch. 8; Heuer & Pherson (2015), Structured Analytic Techniques for Intelligence Analysis. The method's core: judge hypotheses by the evidence against them, because evidence consistent with every hypothesis proves nothing.
  • Implemented. Hypotheses and evidence items as view roles; every item rated against every hypothesis (CC very consistent, C, N neutral or not applicable, I, II very inconsistent) with its reasoning and provenance; each item's credibility and relevance. Hypotheses ranked by weighted inconsistency; items rated identically against every hypothesis flagged as non-diagnostic; linchpins, items without which the lead changes or ties, found by leaving each out in turn, with whether their own sources (the item's citations) were checked.
  • Limits. Ratings and weights are judgements. The weights, high 1.0, medium 0.7, low 0.4 (credibility × relevance), an inconsistency 1 and a strong one 2, are Latris's stated convention, not Heuer's. Items are treated as independent, and no probabilities are computed: the ranking orders hypotheses, it doesn't say how likely each is.

Decision trees and the value of information

  • Lineage. Raiffa (1968), Decision Analysis; Clemen & Reilly (2014), Making Hard Decisions; the value of information after Howard (1966).
  • Implemented. Decision, chance and terminal nodes as view roles; branches as links, each with a label, a probability (from chance nodes) and an optional value along it; payoffs at terminals; maximise or minimise. Rollback by expected value to the optimal strategy and each first choice's value. The expected value of perfect information for each uncertainty, chance nodes that resolve the same uncertainty under different choices are grouped by name and must agree on its states and probabilities, and for all of them together (treated as independent). For each two-state uncertainty, the probabilities at which the best first choice changes, found by scanning and bisection. Every value keeps its provenance; unverified values on the best path are named.
  • Limits. Risk-neutral (no utility function). Probabilities and payoffs are point values unless given a range for the probabilistic sensitivity analysis (below). EVPI is undefined for an uncertainty whose probabilities depend on the choice (it is reported as not priced), and is an upper bound, imperfect information (EVSI) is not computed. A node has one parent; shared subtrees are repeated.

Markov cohort models and cost-effectiveness

  • Lineage. Sonnenberg & Beck (1993), "Markov Models in Medical Decision Making"; Briggs, Claxton & Sculpher (2006), Decision Modelling for Health Economic Evaluation; incremental analysis after Drummond et al. (2015).
  • Implemented. States and strategies as view roles; per-cycle transition probabilities on links (for every strategy, or overridden for one), the rest staying; per-state cost per cycle and utility per year (likewise); the starting cohort. The cohort trace; discounted costs and outcomes (separate rates allowed) with optional half-cycle correction, the trapezoid rule, discounting at mid-cycle; expected time in each state. Incremental analysis: dominated and extendedly dominated strategies, ICERs along the frontier, and net monetary benefit at a willingness to pay. Every value keeps its provenance.
  • Limits. Memoryless, the chance of moving depends only on the current state (no tunnel states or time-varying probabilities yet). Values are point estimates unless given a range for the probabilistic sensitivity analysis (below). A cohort expectation, not individual simulation; MDPs (choosing actions in each state) are not implemented.

Argument maps

  • Lineage. Toulmin (1958), The Uses of Argument (claim, grounds, warrant, qualifier, rebuttal); IBIS, Kunz & Rittel (1970); abstract argumentation and grounded semantics, Dung (1995); support relations (bipolar argumentation), Cayrol & Lagasquie-Schiex (2005); undercutting defeaters, Pollock (1987).
  • Implemented. A conclusion, claims and an optional question as view roles; a link from a premise to a claim supports or attacks it, belongs to a named reason (premises in one reason work only together) and can carry a warrant and qualifier. Grounded labelling with support, to a fixed point: a claim is in when every attack on it fails and a reason for it holds (all its premises in, its inference unchallenged) or it is a premise taken as given (not argued further, not rejected, its sources not against it); out when an attack succeeds, a person rejected it, or every reason fails; otherwise undecided. An open challenge on a link suspends that inference. Objections and answers are told apart by side (attacks on claims that help the conclusion are objections). Linchpins by leave-one-out; the premises the conclusion rests on classified as checked, cited, accepted or unsourced from their citations.
  • Limits. Qualitative: qualifiers are reported, not weighed, and one reason that holds is enough, no numeric strengths, probabilities or preference orderings between arguments (no value-based or structured argumentation such as ASPIC+). A premise not argued further stands by default; the report lists those without a checked source. One conclusion per map.

Equation models

  • Lineage. Dimensional analysis after Buckingham (1914) and Bridgman (1922): a physical equation must balance in its units. Propagating distributions through a model by Monte Carlo after JCGM 101:2008 (Supplement 1 to the Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement). One-at-a-time sensitivity and its limits after Saltelli et al. (2008).
  • Implemented. Inputs, parameters, computed quantities and outputs as view roles; each input and parameter a value with units, a source and an optional valid range; each computed quantity a formula with declared units and a source. Formulas are evaluated in dependency order with every value carrying its units (pint), so a formula that is dimensionally inconsistent, or gives other units than it declares, is refused. Named scenarios; a sweep of one input across a range with the output's shape and its elasticity at the reference value; Monte Carlo over the stated ranges with Spearman rank correlations for what drives the spread. A value outside its stated valid range is reported in every result and never clamped.
  • Limits. Explicit formulas only: a circular set is refused, not solved, and there are no lookup tables or time-varying inputs (system dynamics has both). Dimensional consistency is necessary, not sufficient: it does not make a formula right, and an empirical relationship is only as good as the study behind it, inside the range that study covered. Sweeps move one input at a time and miss interactions; the Monte Carlo samples ranges independently. Units with an offset (°C, °F) can't be used in arithmetic.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis (decision trees and Markov models)

  • Lineage. Briggs, Claxton & Sculpher (2006), Decision Modelling for Health Economic Evaluation, ch. 4–7; acceptability curves after Fenwick, Claxton & Sculpher (2001); the expected value of perfect information over the parameters after Claxton (1999); partial EVPI after Strong, Oakley & Brennan (2014); correlated draws after Iman & Conover (1982); Latin hypercube sampling after McKay, Beckman & Conover (1979).
  • Implemented. Ranges on a decision tree's payoffs, branch values and probabilities (one branch of a two-branch chance node, or every branch as a Dirichlet worth n observations; chance nodes resolving the same uncertainty take the same sampled probabilities), and on a Markov model's transition probabilities, costs and utilities (each for every strategy or one; a state's transitions out as a Dirichlet). Beta and gamma by mean and standard deviation (method of moments), or any of uniform, triangular, normal and lognormal; each range keeps its reason, source and verification and must include the current value. Seeded Latin hypercube sampling through each model's own calculation. Decision trees: each first choice's expected value across the runs, how often each is best, and the EVPI over the parameters. Markov: each strategy's cost and outcome across the runs, the incremental analysis on the means, the cost-effectiveness acceptability curve and EVPI over a willingness-to-pay grid, and the probability the chosen strategy is cost-effective at the stated willingness to pay. Spearman rank correlation names the inputs driving the decision (the best option's advantage, or its net monetary benefit, over the runner-up). The partial EVPI of each input, what knowing it alone before choosing is worth, comes from the same runs, without rerunning the model: each option's net benefit is regressed on the input (Strong, Oakley & Brennan 2014), a cubic spline in its rank for a single value, a quadratic in its components for a set of probabilities. The estimate is cross-fitted: the option to choose at each run's value comes from a regression on the other four fifths of the runs and is paid that run's own net benefit, so the regression's noise can't pass for value. An estimate within sampling noise of zero (under 1.6 standard errors) is reported as zero. Two ranged values can be given a rank correlation (two estimates from one study): Iman–Conover reordering pairs their draws to it while each keeps exactly its own distribution and Latin hypercube strata; a set of correlations that can't hold together is refused.
  • Limits. Correlations join single values, not Dirichlet sets. The partial EVPI is an estimate from the PSA runs, a little low, since the rule for choosing is learnt from a finite number of them, and zero for an input whose value can't be told from noise at that number of runs (more runs can show a small one), and prices one input at a time; sample information (EVSI) is not computed. Separately sampled probabilities of leaving a Markov state that sum past 1 are scaled down to 1, and the report counts how often; a Dirichlet over the state avoids it.

Global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices, Morris screening)

  • Lineage. Saltelli et al. (2008), Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer; variance-based indices after Sobol (2001), estimated as in Saltelli et al. (2010) (first order) and Jansen (1999) (total); elementary effects after Morris (1991), with μ* after Campolongo, Cariboni & Saltelli (2007).
  • Implemented. For fault trees, bow-ties, event trees, decision trees, Markov models, system dynamics and Bayesian networks, every input with a range (the same distributions their Monte Carlo or PSA samples) is varied together on the unit hypercube and mapped through its quantile function. Sobol: a scrambled Sobol sequence, n·(k + 2) runs, first-order and total indices for every output with bootstrap 95% intervals. Morris: r trajectories on a four-level grid over each input's central 95%, r·(k + 1) runs, μ, μ and σ with a bootstrap interval on μ. The headline output is the model's main result (the top event, the best option's advantage over the runner-up, the net monetary benefit at the willingness to pay, an SD variable's metric, a Bayesian network's posterior). A set of probabilities, a chance node's branches, a state's transitions out, a Bayesian-network table worth n observations, is one input spanning several columns, decoded into an exact Dirichlet draw (each component a gamma quantile, normalised) and indexed as a group (Jacques, Lavergne & Devictor 2006). Implemented on numpy and scipy rather than SALib; checked against the Ishigami function's analytic indices and closed forms.
  • Limits. Inputs must be independent (correlations between ranges are ignored by this analysis); a group's index covers the whole set, not each of its rows. Index estimates carry sampling error, with heavy-tailed inputs or small n they can stray outside [0, 1]; the intervals say how far to trust them. Morris effects are per unit of an input's quantile and comparable only within one result.

Theory of change

  • Lineage. Weiss (1995), "Nothing as Practical as Good Theory", a theory of change states how an intervention is expected to work, step by step, with its assumptions made explicit; the logic-model levels follow the W.K. Kellogg Foundation (2004), Logic Model Development Guide; reading the chain link by link against the evidence follows contribution analysis (Mayne 2012, Evaluation 18(3)).
  • Implemented. Six levels as view roles, input, activity, output, short-term outcome, long-term outcome, impact (the outcome of interest). Readiness requires a level on every factor, no link back to an earlier level, an activity and an impact, every impact reached from an activity and no dead ends. latris toc check enumerates every simple pathway from an activity to an impact (up to 200) and gives each link an evidence status, checked (a source read and found to support it), cited (a source not yet checked), untested (none) or contested (a source against it), with its count of stated assumptions; a pathway takes its weakest link's status. Links that skip two or more levels are flagged as leaps. The result is recorded and goes out of date when sources, checks or assumptions change.
  • Process tracing. Bayesian process tracing (Bennett 2015; Befani & Mayne 2014; Fairfield & Charman 2017) weighs how far the evidence supports each link. A link has a prior (default 0.5) and evidence tests, each with P(E | link holds): its certainty, and P(E | it doesn't), whose complement is its uniqueness. latris toc trace multiplies the link's odds by P(E|H)/P(E|¬H) for each test passed and (1−P(E|H))/(1−P(E|¬H)) for each failed, classifies each test by Van Evera's (1997) four types (hoop: certain, P(E|H) ≥ 0.8; smoking gun: unique, P(E|¬H) ≤ 0.2; doubly decisive: both; straw in the wind: neither), gives each pathway the product of its links' posteriors, and ranks tests still to run by how far they could move their link. Probabilities are kept open (strictly between 0 and 1), so no single test settles a link outright.
  • Impact. latris toc impact estimates the change in an outcome from a registered dataset. Without a comparison group it is an interrupted time series: segmented regression (Wagner et al. 2002; Bernal, Cummins & Gasparrini 2017) with a step and change of slope at the start, AR(1) errors by iterated GLS (Prais & Winsten 1954), in simulation, Newey–West errors covered well under 95% on series this short, and the effect by the last period against the projected pre-period trend. With one it is difference-in-differences (Angrist & Pischke 2009): the interaction of group and period, cluster-robust by group when there are 20 or more groups (Bertrand, Duflo & Mullainathan 2004), after a test of whether the groups' pre-period trends differ. Each check that fails or can't be made, fewer than 8 periods a side, strong autocorrelation in a short series, diverging pre-trends, too few groups to cluster, uneven spacing, is reported as a caution.
  • Limits. The check and process tracing are structural and evidential: they show where a results chain rests on untested or contested steps, not how large an effect is. The impact estimate attributes a change to the intervention only under its design's assumptions (no other change at the same time; parallel trends), which the data test only in part; it has no seasonal terms, covariates, staggered starts or synthetic controls. Process tracing is only as good as its probability judgements; it treats tests on a link as independent given the link, and links on a pathway as independent of each other. Feedback within a programme (a ToC is kept acyclic) belongs in a CLD on the same substrate.

The evidence discipline

  • Lineage. The substrate's vocabulary (claims, warrants, rebuttals) is Toulmin's (1958), The Uses of Argument. The wider tradition of marshalling evidence for and against a claim includes Wigmore (1937) and Schum (1994). The practice of recording which part of a source was read and whether it supports the claim follows the evidence-assessment tradition of Bradford Hill (1965) and the grading of evidence quality in GRADE (Guyatt et al. 2008). These are inspirations for the audit trail, not implemented criteria: Latris does not score a link against the Bradford Hill checklist, and it does not assign GRADE ratings.
  • Implemented. Source-vs-claim checks with verdicts (latris evidence check: supports / contradicts / orthogonal / insufficient extract); an AI's check must quote the source and the quote is looked up in the text Latris holds (latris ref snapshot); a paraphrase is refused; every check records who made it and the source's access tier (title, abstract, extract, full text). Contested links keep sources for and against on the same link and report both. Challenges are an adversarial review log with a conversation and a terminal state.
  • Limits. A Toulmin/IBIS argument-map model type is registered (latris capabilities lists it) but not planned; the substrate borrows the vocabulary, it does not implement the formalism. Checks are only as good as the stored source text; a check against an abstract cannot speak for the full text.

Where each formalism sits on the ladder of causation

Pearl & Mackenzie (2018) order causal questions as association → intervention → counterfactual. Honest placement:

Formalism Position
CLD Mechanism claims: qualitative structure only; arrows are argued, not estimated
FCM Semi-quantitative simulation of the claimed mechanism
SD Quantitative mechanism simulation; scenario analysis intervenes on the model
Theory of Change Mechanism claims laid out as a results chain, each step read against its evidence: with process tracing, weighed by Bayes' rule; with data, an interrupted-time-series or difference-in-differences impact estimate, valid only under that design's assumptions
Fault Tree Logical decomposition of how a failure can occur; probabilities assume independent basic events, optionally with Monte Carlo over their uncertainty: no causal estimation
Bow-Tie Barrier structure around one event; LOPA frequencies are order-of-magnitude, barriers assumed independent
Event Tree Forward sequences from one event; probabilities are as conditional as you state them: no causal estimation
MCDA Not causal: a structured comparison of options under stated weights
ACH Weighs explanations against evidence; orders hypotheses, gives no probabilities
Decision tree Choices under stated probabilities; the value of knowing them first: not causal estimation
Markov model Expected costs and outcomes under stated transition probabilities: not causal estimation
Argument map Whether a conclusion stands given the reasons and objections stated, and what it rests on: qualitative, not a probability
Equation model Quantitative mechanism, computed from stated formulas; changing an input intervenes on the model, and the answer is only as causal as the formulas' sources make it
DAG / BN Observational queries and identification: what to measure; with data, an average effect by back-door adjusted regression, valid only under the DAG's assumptions; no counterfactuals

A CLD arrow is not a structural equation and carries no do-semantics; a successful scenario run says what the model does under an assumed change, not what the world would do. Reports should be quoted with that in mind, and Latris reports say it themselves in the Methods section.

Deliberately not implemented

Black-box machine learning and opaque structure learning (they fail the inspectability test: latris capabilities, excluded_by_design); counterfactual estimation, and interventional estimation beyond back-door adjusted regression on a DAG and the two quasi-experimental impact designs on a theory of change (interrupted time series, difference-in-differences). XMILE/Vensim import is no longer excluded, it joined the roadmap on 2026-11-25 (see ROADMAP.md); it had never been an inspectability objection. See the capability manifest for the living list.
