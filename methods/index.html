Scientific basis

Established methods, cited only where they're implemented.

Latris doesn't invent a causal calculus, a simulation algorithm, a decision rule or a theory of evidence. It implements well-known ones from system dynamics, graphical causal inference, Bayesian networks, programme evaluation, reliability engineering, decision analysis and argumentation, records which were used in each model, and states their limits next to their results.

If you've been building these models for years, the pages below are written for you: what each model type's links mean in Latris, where the method comes from, the problems practitioners already know about and what Latris does about each, and what it deliberately doesn't do.

Model types

Fifteen model types, one evidence base.

All of them are built on the same factors, links and evidence, so a model can be quantified, or examined another way, without being redrawn. How Latris cites

The citation rule.

A work is cited as the basis of a feature only where Latris implements the method it describes: not the whole book, and never a method that is only planned. Where an idea is inspiration rather than implementation, the text says so. On these pages, every reference is marked Implemented or Context.

Every Latris report carries a methods section generated from one registry, citing only the methods that model actually used, with their limits. If you find a citation that overclaims, that's a bug: please report it.

Checked against the literature

Replications.

Latris is checked against textbook models with known results: the Asia diagnostic network, Bass diffusion, the Kermack and McKendrick epidemic, Pearl's back-door example and Senge's system archetypes. Each is rebuilt from the original, and every number Latris computes is compared with what that model is known to give.

They ship with the library, so you don't have to take our word for it: latris replicate check reruns them all. See every check and its result.

Across every model type

The evidence discipline.

What sets Latris apart isn't a new method; it's that every link in every model type is treated as a claim with evidence. The vocabulary of claims, warrants and rebuttals is Toulmin's (1958). The wider tradition of marshalling evidence for and against a claim runs through Wigmore (1937) and Schum (1994), and the practice of recording what was read and whether it supports the claim draws on Bradford Hill (1965) and GRADE (Guyatt et al. 2008).

These are inspirations for the audit trail, not implemented criteria: Latris doesn't score links against the Bradford Hill viewpoints or assign GRADE ratings. What it implements is the record. A source is checked against the specific claim it's cited for, with a verdict; the check records who made it and how much of the source they could see (title, abstract, extract or full text); an AI's check must quote the source, and the quote is looked up in the text Latris holds. Sources for and against stay on the same link, and challenges keep dissent on the record until someone resolves them.

A check is only as good as the text behind it: a check against an abstract can't speak for the full paper, and the report says which one it rests on.

Causal claims

Where each model type sits on the ladder of causation.

Pearl and Mackenzie (2018) order causal questions as association, intervention and counterfactual. Latris places each model type honestly, including the ones that make no causal estimate at all, and its reports say the same in their methods section.

Model typeWhat it can support
Causal loop diagramClaims about mechanism: qualitative structure only. Arrows are argued, not estimated, and carry no interventional semantics.
Fuzzy cognitive mapA semi-quantitative simulation of the claimed mechanism.
System dynamicsA quantitative simulation of the claimed mechanism. Scenarios intervene on the model, not the world.
Causal DAGIdentification: what would have to be measured for an effect to be estimable. With data, an average effect by adjusted regression, valid only under the graph's assumptions. No counterfactuals.
Bayesian networkObservational queries: what is likely, given what has been observed.
Equation modelA quantitative mechanism computed from stated formulas. Changing an input intervenes on the model, and the answer is only as causal as the formulas' sources make it.
Theory of changeClaims about mechanism laid out as a results chain, each step read against its evidence. With data, an impact estimate valid only under its design's assumptions.
Fault tree, bow-tie, event treeThe logic of how a failure occurs, what protects against it and what follows, under stated probabilities. No causal estimation.
Decision tree, Markov modelExpected outcomes of choices under stated probabilities. Not causal estimation.
Multi-criteria decision analysisNot causal: a structured comparison of options under stated weights.
Analysis of competing hypothesesWeighs explanations against evidence. Orders hypotheses and gives no probabilities.
Argument mapWhether a conclusion stands given the reasons and objections stated. Qualitative, not a probability.

The methods reference has the same table in full, and each model type has its own page under every model type.

Good modelling practice

Purpose, context and evaluation, recorded with the model.

Environmental modellers will know the ten iterative steps of Jakeman, Letcher and Norton (2006): define the purpose, specify the context, conceptualise the system, choose the model features, and so on through to evaluation and documentation. Latris doesn't implement a procedure, but it records most of what those steps ask for as part of the model itself: the background and the decision it informs, the audience, the boundary, what the person expected the answer to be, assumptions, deliberate exclusions, decisions with their rationale, predictions to check later, and the sensitivity analyses run.

What it doesn't yet do is evaluate a model's performance against data: there's no calibration, no behaviour-reproduction testing and no predictive validation. The model-type pages say where that bites.

By design

What Latris won't do.

Black-box predictors you can run but can't inspect or defend (random forests, gradient boosting, neural networks) and opaque structure learning are excluded by design: a method is admitted only if its reasoning stays inspectable. Interventional and counterfactual estimation on DAGs isn't implemented, and Vensim and XMILE import is off the roadmap.

Check it yourself

Every algorithm is open.

The engine that runs every analysis is open source under Apache-2.0, with a test suite of over 2,000 tests and a worked example that exercises every command end to end. You can read exactly how loop polarity, adjustment sets, soft evidence or loop dominance are computed.
