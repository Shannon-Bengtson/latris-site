How it works

Most of the reasoning never makes it into the model. Latris keeps it.

The diagram is the easy part. What decides whether anyone trusts the result is everything behind it: the evidence for each link, what you considered and ruled out, the assumptions you'd have to defend in a meeting. That usually lives across five tools, or in your head, and six months later nobody can say why a particular arrow is on the page.

AI has made this sharper. When a model takes an afternoon to generate, every relationship needs a reason to exist. Latris records the reason when the arrow is drawn, and keeps the disagreement on the record when there is one.

The workflow

Five phases, one model.

Data and references sit alongside every phase, so evidence can come in whenever you find it. Traffic-light readiness checks between phases show what's missing before you move on.

1

Build

Lay out the factors and the links between them. Stage candidates, edit on the canvas, or review everything in a table.

2

Formalise

Choose the model type the question needs: a causal map taken as far as you can defend (direction, weights, probabilities, full system dynamics), a theory of change, a decision or risk model, an argument map, or sourced equations. Which model?

3

Analyse

Feedback loops and effect paths, simulations and scenarios, rankings and expected values, cut sets and frequencies, uncertainty and sensitivity, each recorded with its result.

4

Interpret

Turn results into an answer, implications, confidence and open questions, tied to the claims the model actually supports.

5

Communicate

A hosted report, a Word document, a review pack for outside reviewers, a plain-language story, and diagram exports.

Evidence

Every link is a claim, with its evidence attached.

A source doesn't vouch for a whole diagram. In Latris it attaches to a specific claim, such as "more riparian cover means lower nutrient loads", and it can be cited for that claim or against it.

Each check of a source records who checked it, what they could see (the title, the abstract or the full text), whether the source really supports the claim, and the passage it rests on. When an AI quotes a source, the quote is looked up in the text Latris holds; a paraphrase is refused. Until someone checks, the report says the source is "not yet checked against the source".

Around the links sit the rest of the reasoning: assumptions, decisions, open questions, deliberate exclusions, predictions and sign-offs, each part of the record rather than a note in a margin.

AI

AI drafts. People decide, and the record shows which is which.

AI is useful for the parts that take the time: candidate factors and links, sources to check, counterarguments, evidence gaps, and first drafts of the report's prose. Latris lets it do that work without letting it pass as yours.

Everything an AI adds is recorded as AI-proposed, and evidence it attaches stays unverified until a person reviews it. The report carries those labels, findings show whether a person approved them, and every change stays in the model's history.

The AI is your choice: Claude or ChatGPT through the Latris connector, an agent driving the open-source CLI, or your own API key. Latris itself doesn't send your models to AI providers.

Disagreement

Contested links stay contested.

When sources disagree, both sides stay on the same link and the report lists them under "Contested links". When a person disputes a factor or a link, that's a challenge, and its outcome is recorded: kept unchanged, kept with a caveat, modified, split, removed, or still unresolved.

The diagram shows it too: solid lines for established links, dashed for ones still argued, and a mark on any link where sources disagree or someone has challenged it.

Before it goes out

A checklist for what's left.

Before a model goes to a board, Latris lists what still needs attention, each with a way to fix it: links with no source, citations nobody has checked, quotes that can't be found in the source's text, findings nobody has approved, report sections that are missing or out of date because the model changed, and contradictions.

Made to be handed over.

Most modelling tools assume you'll be next to the screen, explaining. Latris assumes you won't be. Every output comes from the same model, so there's one source of truth.

Interactive report

A link that opens the typeset diagram, result charts and interpretation. Readers click any arrow for its evidence. No account needed to read it.

Word report

The written case with every claim tied to its evidence, for organisations that live in documents.

Review pack

A single file reviewers open in a browser to add challenges, questions and sign-offs, then send back. For reviewers

Plain-language story

A scrolling page that answers your question for the audience you choose: councillors, a community, a board. The AI drafts the outline from what the model records, you approve it, and the model's picture follows the words. Every claim traces back to the model. Share it by link; readers need no account.

Reports come in versions for different readers: a client report that leads with the answer, plus publication, review, workshop and audit versions. Every report includes a methods section that cites only the methods the model actually used.

History

Every change, kept.

Each model is a git repository. Every change is a recorded, reversible step with a name on it, so you can see who changed what, when, and which version backed a decision. Changes made with AI help say so in the record.
