Effect estimate

Run 2026-11-23 19:49 UTC.

Effect of Air pollution on Respiratory hospital admissions (admissions, n=400, adjusting for Traffic restrictions, Weather and season): +0.3212 in Respiratory hospital admissions per unit of Air pollution (95% CI 0.232 to 0.4103).

Adjusted for Traffic restrictions, Weather and season — the smallest valid backdoor set the dataset measures. Model: linear regression, HC3 robust standard errors; 400 complete rows from admissions.

Change in Respiratory hospital admissions per unit of Air pollution 95% CI p
Adjusted 0.3212 0.232 – 0.4103 1.652e-12
Unadjusted 0.2832 0.2313 – 0.3351 1.053e-26

This is the effect only if: The DAG is right: no common cause of the treatment and the outcome is missing from it. The model's form is right: the outcome depends linearly (or, for a yes/no outcome, logistically) and additively on the adjustment variables. Positivity: units with every combination of the adjustment variables could receive every treatment level. Rows with a missing value are left out (complete-case analysis), which is unbiased only if they are missing for reasons unrelated to the outcome.

Methods and foundations

Established methods this model relies on, with their sources and stated limits. Latris cites a method only where it implements it — see the foundations reference for the full account.

  • Causal identification on a DAG — d-separation queries, Markov blankets, path enumeration, and backdoor adjustment sets by graph surgery (Pearl (2009), Causality; Hernán & Robins (2020), Causal Inference: What If; d-separation after Shachter (1998)). Limit: Identification: says what must be adjusted for, not how big the effect is (that is the separate effect estimate); no counterfactuals; minimal adjustment sets are enumerated only up to size 3.
  • Effect estimation by backdoor adjustment — the average effect of one factor on another from a dataset: the smallest valid backdoor set made only of measured factors (or a given set, checked against the DAG), linear regression with robust standard errors or logistic regression for a yes/no outcome, beside the unadjusted association (Pearl (2009), Causality, the back-door adjustment; Hernán & Robins (2020), Causal Inference: What If, ch. 15 (outcome regression); MacKinnon & White (1985), HC3 standard errors; Seabold & Perktold (2010), statsmodels). Limit: Causal only under the DAG's assumptions (no common cause left out), the model's linear, additive form, and positivity; complete cases only; one numeric or two-valued treatment, no effect modification; refuses rather than estimates when every valid adjustment set needs an unmeasured factor.
  • Source-vs-claim checking with a verification audit trail — recording whether a cited source supports the claim, who checked, which part of the source was read, and keeping sources for and against together on contested links (practice after Bradford Hill (1965) and GRADE (Guyatt et al. 2008); argumentation vocabulary from Toulmin (1958) — inspiration, not implemented criteria). Limit: Does not score claims against the Bradford Hill criteria and does not assign GRADE ratings; a check is only as good as the stored source text.
