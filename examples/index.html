A real model, not a demo

Is this river getting sick, and where would restoration money do the most good?

A causal loop diagram of a regional Australian river, prepared for a catchment management board deciding where to spend. It was built entirely in Latris from public sources: the national State of the Environment report and the published literature on riverside vegetation.

What the model says

Nutrient loads are the pressure to act on first. They drive the algal blooms, the blooms cut dissolved oxygen as they decompose, and low oxygen is the documented killer in Australian fish deaths. Blooms and turbidity feed each other, which is why a river can hold itself in a poor state.

Riverside (riparian) vegetation is the one lever that acts on both paths at once. The honest caveat sits on that link: the evidence says buffers work, but their real-world effectiveness varies widely. So the model supports a monitored pilot reach, not a catchment-wide planting program on day one.

The board expected "plant the banks and wait". The report records that expectation next to the answer, so the difference is visible.
