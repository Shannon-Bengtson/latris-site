For decision owners and reviewers

You sign off the model. You should be able to read it.

If you commission, review or inherit model-backed work, you carry the risk without having built the thing. Latris puts the reasoning where you can check it, and keeps it there after the people who built it have moved on.

At sign-off

The questions that matter, answered in the report.

What does this rest on?

Every link shows its reason and its sources, and whether each source was checked against what it actually says.

What did AI write?

AI contributions are labelled as AI-proposed. You can see which were reviewed by a person and which weren't.

Where do the sources disagree?

Contested links list the sources for and against, and any open challenge, instead of quietly picking a side.

What drove the result?

Assumptions, exclusions and decisions are recorded with their reasons, next to the result they shaped.

Who changed what, and when?

The full history is kept automatically, so you can tell which version backed the decision.

Could someone else pick this up?

In eighteen months, another team can see not only the model but why it looks the way it does.

Review packs

Challenge it, question it, sign it off.

A review pack is a single file you open in a browser. No account and no install. You can add a challenge, a question or a sign-off against the whole project, a single factor or a single link, with your name and role.

Save the file and send it back. Your feedback comes into the model's record as challenges, questions and sign-offs, so it doesn't get lost in an email thread. If the model changed after the pack was sent, Latris warns that your comments may refer to an older version.

Reading a report link needs no account either: open it, click any arrow, and see the evidence.

Findings carry their approval and the evidence behind them; contested links show both sides.

Methods

Established methods, with their limits stated.

Latris doesn't invent its methods. It implements established ones, cites them only where it actually implements them, and states the limits next to the results. A causal DAG analysis, for example, is labelled as identification, never as an estimated effect.
