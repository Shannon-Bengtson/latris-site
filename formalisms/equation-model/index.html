The methods, their references and their limits

Equation models: sourced formulas with dimensional analysis

Used for: evaluating formulas in dependency order with every value carrying its units, so a dimensionally inconsistent formula is refused; comparing named scenarios; one-at-a-time sweeps of an input with the elasticity at the reference value; and Monte Carlo propagation of the stated ranges through the formulas, with rank correlations for what drives the spread.

Limits: Explicit formulas only: a circular set is refused, not solved, and there are no lookup tables or time-varying inputs. Dimensional consistency does not make a formula right, and an empirical formula is only as good as its source: values outside a stated valid range are reported, not corrected. Sweeps move one input at a time and miss interactions; Monte Carlo samples the ranges independently.

References: Buckingham (1914), "On Physically Similar Systems", Physical Review 4(4); Bridgman (1922), Dimensional Analysis; Saltelli et al. (2008), Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer, ch. 1-2; JCGM 101:2008, Propagation of distributions using a Monte Carlo method

Every number carries where it came from, and a value nobody has verified is flagged where the result rests on it. Every method Latris implements is in the methods reference.
