The methods, their references and their limits

Bow-tie barrier analysis with layer-of-protection estimates

Used for: threat and consequence lines through prevention and mitigation barriers, escalation factors and their controls, the CCPS barrier types; flags for lines resting on one effective barrier or none, barriers shared between lines, uncontrolled escalation and barriers with no owner; with threat frequencies and barrier failure probabilities, the top-event and consequence frequencies and how much each barrier buys.

Limits: Barriers are assumed to fail independently (shared barriers are flagged, and counted once per threat); frequencies and failure probabilities are point values at the order-of-magnitude precision of LOPA; escalation factors are not quantified and there is no consequence severity or risk matrix.

References: CCPS & Energy Institute (2018), Bow Ties in Risk Management: A Concept Book for Process Safety; CCPS (2001), Layer of Protection Analysis: Simplified Process Risk Assessment

Global sensitivity analysis: Sobol indices and Morris screening

Used for: apportioning the variance of a result among every input with a range, varied together over their distributions: first-order and total Sobol indices (Saltelli and Jansen estimators on a scrambled Sobol sequence, bootstrap intervals), or Morris elementary effects (μ*, σ) as a cheaper screen.

Limits: Inputs are independent (correlations between ranges are ignored here); a set of probabilities (a Dirichlet: a chance node's branches, a state's transitions out, a Bayesian-network table) is one input, indexed as a group; indices are estimates, with heavy-tailed inputs or few samples they can stray below 0 or above 1 (the intervals say how far to trust them); Morris effects are per unit of an input's quantile, comparable within one result only.

References: Saltelli et al. (2008), Global Sensitivity Analysis: The Primer; Sobol (2001), Mathematics and Computers in Simulation 55; Saltelli et al. (2010), Computer Physics Communications 181(2); Jansen (1999), Computer Physics Communications 117; Morris (1991), Technometrics 33(2); Campolongo, Cariboni & Saltelli (2007), Environmental Modelling & Software 22(10)

Monte Carlo propagation of parameter uncertainty in risk models

Used for: how sure a fault tree's top-event probability, a bow-tie's frequencies or an event tree's end-state frequencies are: a distribution (or a lognormal median and error factor) on each uncertain input, seeded Latin hypercube sampling through the model's own calculation, percentiles and the mean against the point estimate, and Spearman rank correlation for what drives the spread.

Limits: Inputs are sampled independently, except that each input takes one value per run wherever it appears (state-of-knowledge dependence within a model); no correlation between different inputs. Inputs without a range are treated as exact, so the spread is a lower bound. Rank correlation measures monotone influence only.

References: US Nuclear Regulatory Commission (1983), PRA Procedures Guide (NUREG/CR-2300), ch. 12 (uncertainty, lognormal error factors); Apostolakis (1990), "The Concept of Probability in Safety Assessments of Technological Systems", Science 250; McKay, Beckman & Conover (1979), Latin hypercube sampling; Helton & Davis (2003), "Latin hypercube sampling and the propagation of uncertainty in analyses of complex systems", Reliability Engineering & System Safety 81

Every number carries where it came from, and a value nobody has verified is flagged where the result rests on it. Every method Latris implements is in the methods reference.
