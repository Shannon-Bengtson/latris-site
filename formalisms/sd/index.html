System dynamics

Stocks, flows and feedback, with every parameter sourced.

System dynamics simulates how accumulations and feedback produce behaviour over time. It asks the most of a modeller: equations, units and numbers you'd defend in front of a reviewer. Latris gives every one of those numbers a place to record where it came from.

At a glance

The model claimsStocks accumulate their net flows; flows and auxiliaries are functions of the system's state, written as equations with units.
NumbersInitial values, constants, lookup tables and equations, all unit-checked.
OutputTrajectories over time, under scenarios, with uncertainty bands, sensitivities and the loops that drive the behaviour.
Causal standingQuantitative simulation of the claimed mechanism. A scenario intervenes on the model; whether the world would respond the same way depends on the structure being right.
In LatrisUnit-checked simulation, standard delay structures, scenarios with provenance, Latin-hypercube Monte Carlo, one-at-a-time sensitivity, and Loops That Matter.

Where it comes from

System dynamics was founded by Jay Forrester at MIT (Industrial Dynamics, 1961). Sterman's Business Dynamics (2000) is the field's standard text, and Latris's delay structures follow its chapter on delays (ch. 11). Meadows (2008) carried the ideas to a wide audience. On which loop drives behaviour, Latris implements the Loops That Matter method (Schoenberg, Davidsen and Eberlein 2020, with the revised flow-to-stock score of Schoenberg, Hayward and Eberlein 2023), the approach Stella and PySimlin also use. Uncertainty sampling uses Latin hypercubes (McKay, Beckman and Conover 1979).

The field's long-standing position on validity is that a model can't be proven true, only tested until it earns confidence, and that tests of structure come before tests of behaviour (Forrester and Senge 1980; Barlas 1996; Sterman 2000, ch. 21). That framing is how to read what Latris does and doesn't do below.

What practitioners know to watch for

The known problemWhat Latris does
Dimensional errors. Equations that don't balance their units are a common structural fault, and easy to miss by eye. Every equation is unit-checked (with base dimensions for people, money and counted items). An equation whose units don't match its declared units stops the run with an error naming it.
Parameters nobody can source. A model's numbers often outlive the memory of where they came from. Parameters sit on factors and links that carry reasons and sources, scenario values carry their own reason, and sensitivity results flag values nobody has reviewed.
Point estimates hiding uncertainty. One run says nothing about how sure the answer is. Uncertain constants and initial values take distributions (uniform, triangular, normal, lognormal, beta, gamma). Seeded Latin-hypercube Monte Carlo gives percentile bands and rank correlations; one-at-a-time sensitivity gives a tornado chart.
Which loop is in charge? Behaviour shifts as loop dominance shifts, and diagrams can't show it. Loops That Matter scores every feedback loop at every step and flags the loop, or fewest loops, carrying the behaviour.
Delays done crudely. Ad hoc lags misrepresent how information and material move. SMOOTH, SMOOTH3, DELAY1 and DELAY3 are expanded into their standard stock-and-flow structures (Sterman 2000, ch. 11).
Integration artefacts. Step size and method can change results. The clock (time units, start, end, step) and the method (fixed-step Euler or adaptive Runge-Kutta) are stored with the model, so every run is reproducible.

Model testing: what's covered

Against the standard battery of tests, Latris covers the structural end. Dimensional consistency is enforced. Structure verification, whether each relationship reflects the real system, is what the evidence trail is for: every link argued, sourced, checkable and open to challenge. Parameter verification is supported by provenance on every value, and sensitivity by the analyses above. Behaviour-reproduction tests against historical data, extreme-condition tests and calibration are not implemented.

What Latris implements

  • Simulation: stock-and-flow integration by fixed-step Euler or adaptive Runge-Kutta (RK23/RK45), with dimensional analysis on every equation.
  • Functions: STEP, RAMP, PULSE, lookup tables, MIN, MAX, IF_THEN_ELSE and arithmetic; SMOOTH/SMOOTH3 and DELAY1/DELAY3 as stock-and-flow structure.
  • Scenarios: named what-ifs that override constants or initial values, unit-checked and carrying their reason.
  • Uncertainty: seeded Latin-hypercube sampling with percentile bands and Spearman rank correlations.
  • Sensitivity: one-at-a-time swings across each uncertain value's 5th to 95th percentile.
  • Loop dominance: Loops That Matter scores at every step, with dominant loops flagged.

What it doesn't do

  • No calibration to data and no behaviour-reproduction or extreme-condition testing.
  • The adaptive option is RK45 under a step cap, not the classical fixed-step RK4 of Vensim and Stella.
  • Monte Carlo is capped at 1,000 runs and reports rank correlations only. Sensitivity here is one-at-a-time; apportioning the result's variance among the uncertain values is the separate global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices, Morris screening).
  • Loop dominance enumerates loops exhaustively up to 500; larger models would need the strongest-path search, which isn't implemented. Scores are zero when stocks stop changing: the method explains change, not equilibrium.
  • No XMILE or Vensim import.

On the ladder of causation

A simulation is quantitative, but its scenarios intervene on the model, not on the world. What a scenario run shows is what the model does under an assumed change. How far that transfers to the real system depends on the structure, which is why Latris keeps the evidence for every link alongside the results.
