The methods, their references and their limits

Theory of change as a results chain

Used for: logic-model levels (input, activity, output, short- and long-term outcome, impact), every pathway from an activity to an impact, and for each link its sources for and against, whether they were checked, and the assumptions stated for it.

Limits: Structural and evidential only, it shows where a results chain is unsupported, not how large an effect is or how much of an outcome the intervention caused; pathways are enumerated up to 200.

References: Weiss (1995), "Nothing as Practical as Good Theory", in Connell et al. (eds), New Approaches to Evaluating Community Initiatives; W.K. Kellogg Foundation (2004), Logic Model Development Guide; Mayne (2012), "Contribution Analysis: Coming of Age?", Evaluation 18(3)

Impact estimation: interrupted time series and difference-in-differences

Used for: the change in a theory-of-change outcome when the intervention started: with no comparison group, segmented regression (step and change of slope, AR(1) errors, the effect by the last period against the projected pre-period trend); with one, difference-in-differences (cluster-robust errors with 20 or more groups) after comparing the groups' pre-period trends.

Limits: Impact only under the design's assumptions, no other change at the same time (ITS), parallel trends (DiD), which the data can test only in part; no seasonal terms, covariates, staggered starts or synthetic controls; rows are averaged to one value per period (per group). Each failed or impossible check is reported as a caution with the result.

References: Wagner, Soumerai, Zhang & Ross-Degnan (2002), "Segmented regression analysis of interrupted time series studies", J Clin Pharm Ther 27(4); Bernal, Cummins & Gasparrini (2017), "Interrupted time series regression for the evaluation of public health interventions", Int J Epidemiol 46(1); Angrist & Pischke (2009), Mostly Harmless Econometrics, ch. 5; Bertrand, Duflo & Mullainathan (2004), "How Much Should We Trust Differences-in-Differences Estimates?", QJE 119(1); Prais & Winsten (1954), AR(1) errors; Seabold & Perktold (2010), statsmodels

Bayesian process tracing

Used for: how far the evidence supports each link of a theory of change: a prior per link, each test's P(evidence | link holds) and P(evidence | it doesn't), odds-form updating for tests passed or failed, Van Evera's classification of each test, pathway confidence, and which test still to run would move a link most.

Limits: The probabilities are judgements, and the answer is only as good as them; tests on a link are treated as independent given whether it holds, and pathway confidence multiplies link posteriors as if the links were independent. It weighs evidence for a causal step; it doesn't estimate how large the effect is.

References: Van Evera (1997), Guide to Methods for Students of Political Science (hoop, smoking-gun, doubly-decisive and straw-in-the-wind tests); Bennett (2015), "Disciplining Our Conjectures", in Bennett & Checkel (eds), Process Tracing; Befani & Mayne (2014), "Process Tracing and Contribution Analysis", IDS Bulletin 45(6); Fairfield & Charman (2017), "Explicit Bayesian Analysis for Process Tracing", Political Analysis 25(3)
